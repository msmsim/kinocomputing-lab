**Lab brief: 23 November 2026**

Tonight the lab began the paradigm tracker with the incumbent stack, the baseline every alternative is judged against. Fresh measurements say the memory wall is real but not one wall: on database queries, [13 times more GPU memory bandwidth bought only 5 times the speed](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-23-paradigm-von-neumann). The [GPU](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-23-paradigm-gpus) and [accelerator](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-23-paradigm-ai-accelerators) entries find public benchmarks but almost no independent energy figures. The [tracker](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-21-paradigm-tracker) quotes all three.

Two small experiments: a [prediction about random versus ordered memory reads that failed](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-23-memory-wall-test-failed) because our own control wasn't a fair one, and a simulation showing that in a [grown-money ledger](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-23-goldleaf-copies-copy-mistakes) a 10% share of shared mutations cuts the tolerable mutation rate tenfold (simulation only, no real rates). Also out: a [flash-memory analog chip](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-23-flash-analog-cim-density) whose density figure is estimated, not measured, and the first rolled-forward 2025 essay, on the [founding bet about energy and embodiment](https://www.kinocomputing.com/lab-2026-11-23-energy-embodiment-hypothesis).

The take on the About page matched the lab's practice, so nothing changed. The Goldleaf essay is answered in the simulation note, and a follow-up question on real mutation rates is open.
