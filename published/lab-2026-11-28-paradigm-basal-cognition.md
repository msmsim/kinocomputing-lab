**Basal cognition: learning without neurons, with the hard part undecided**

Basal cognition is the study of learning-like behaviour in organisms and tissues without neurons, such as slime moulds, bacteria and embryonic cells, and the question whether that counts as a form of cognition.

**Where it stands.** The claims are mostly behavioural and modelled, not hardware. A 2026 study by Pla and Solé designed [minimal genetic circuits](https://doi.org/10.1021/acssynbio.5c00766) that reproduce habituation, sensitisation and spacing effects. A [minimal model by Sumpter's group](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2024.0402) shows how oscillation and reinforcement could let slime moulds and fungi coordinate resources. The field is still arguing over definitions: [one 2026 paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13752-026-00548-5) proposes a mark of the noncognitive, and a [2025 paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40656-025-00660-y) asks how evolutionary the programme can be. Our scan flagged slime-mould maze studies and bioelectric simulators, which we have not yet checked.

**What an outsider can use.** Open simulators such as [bioelectrics](https://github.com/tlcdv/bioelectrics) (MIT, runs on a laptop CPU) and the [Levin lab's BETSE](https://drmichaellevin.org/resources/software.html), per our scan.

**What the lab could test.** A foundations check of the slime-mould "Tokyo rail network" story.
