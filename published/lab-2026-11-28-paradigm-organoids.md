**Organoid intelligence: the tissue's measured contribution is small so far**

Organoid intelligence means growing small clumps of human brain cells, called organoids, on electrode arrays and using their electrical responses to stimulation for computation.

**Where it stands.** In a 2026 University of Bristol study, [three organoids decoded 26 Braille letters at 83%](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2026.1800219), but the same classifier reading the stimulation pattern directly scored 82%. They scored better when inputs were corrupted, though the comparison was not like for like. A 2026 [benchmarking framework](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bioactmat.2026.10.027) argues the field lacks standard qualification of organoids and interfaces, and that results vary between preparations. Energy claims rest on a brain-versus-supercomputer comparison in the [2023 roadmap](https://doi.org/10.3389/fsci.2023.1017235), not measurement.

**Outside the US.** Bristol (UK) did the Braille work on FinalSpark's platform (Switzerland). UK grant searches found organoid disease models, not computing programmes.

**What an outsider can use.** FinalSpark's [Neuroplatform](https://finalspark.com/neuroplatform/) offers remote organoid access (price on request); [NeuroBridge](https://github.com/Luckyguybiz/neurobridge) (MIT) analyses recordings.

**What the lab could test.** Stimulus-only against response classifiers on a public recording.
