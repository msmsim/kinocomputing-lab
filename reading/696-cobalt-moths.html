A whole lot going on here. First, immediately reminded of the notion of descendability, which I’ve briefly written about; it is accumulated intelligence moving down the stack to become biological sediment. Second, I’m reminded of the idea of sensemaking and the cost of arbitrary queries. To deploy intelligence in complex domains, lots of efforts goes into building up the narrative or story, to ramping up (or in), but once done arbitrary queries can be met with high quality answers at near-zero cost. Third, agreed that the gap between normative spec and behavioural assurance is large. The analogy that seems apt is a body and its skin. The body is this horrifically complex entity that evolves over time, but skin is a remarkably sensitive, low latency, resilient and regenerable layer that lets the body be in the world. Fourth, as Aneesh Sathe mentions, liveness fits into this picture. Seems like engineering intelligence is a necessary but not sufficient condition for the emergence of liveness. The notion of “intelligence is not the bottleneck” in organisations seems applicable. Fifth, on regeneration debt: Eric Raymond coined the idea of a hot state dependency, essentially stating that when a complex system goes down there are attributes and characteristics and causal factors it exhibited that can never be regenerated, not matter the effort provided or their criticality to the previously alive system.

The first generation of software engineering disciplines gave us ways to accumulate increasingly large bodies of intelligence in code. The second gave us ways to keep those bodies of code evolving without losing their accumulated intelligence. A third might give us ways to conserve and evolve engineering intelligence without requiring the preservation of the code in which it was historically embodied.
