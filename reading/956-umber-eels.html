I'd been meaning to dig deeper into the idea of coding agents as optimizing compilers from oracles to production code, and was surprised by how relevant it was to this essay and how much richer a research program the compiler analogy suggests.

One particularly interesting idea is progressive lowering. Modern compilers translate through a series of intermediate representations, each with its own semantics and optimization opportunities. Imagine doing the same with software intent, progressively lowering it through a series of computational worlds, each with its own vocabulary, laws, and verification machinery, until you arrive at a concrete implementation.

TanStack DB, for example, could become a verified compiler target whose laws around reactive queries and transactions can be taken for granted when expressing higher-level application behavior. Someone building orchard management software could construct another world on top of that, with native concepts like cultivars, phenology, and spatial competition, in which expressing and exploring novel orchard designs becomes almost trivial.

The really exciting possibility isn't just cheaper software but an explosion of new computational worlds in which increasingly sophisticated ideas can be trivially and reliably expressed and realized.
