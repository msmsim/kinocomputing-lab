I’d be interested in a measure that evaluates task parallelisation and task scalability on a per paradigm basis, and the unpacked differences.

We can measure this in a clean way. An activity is 2x intrinsic scale if it needs to interrupt the human in the loop 1/2 as much, allowing you to run 2 such threads with the same attention. This means it’s doing more routine things that require human design/taste calls half as often. Which means, in a sense it’s operating half as smart. Now it could be that it’s making superintelligent genius decisions on its own, and only interrupting the human for human-level decisions. But thus seems unlikely. If I’m running a swarm to solve the Riemann Hypothesis, which I’m not competent to run at all, it would be weird if it stopped to check on decisions that are within my math abilities. Impedance matching the specific human in the loop, chances are it is doing horizontal scale-out at about the level the human could do or lower. Ie it’s like hundreds or thousands of copies of that particular human’s abilities (within 0.5x-2x say) going relentlessly beyond that human’s stamina limits.

The conclusion from this argument is that the really large-scale orchestration stunts are likely fairly dumb overall. This shouldn’t be surprising. In scaling manufacturing, learning mostly happens early in the ramp. Scaling is when you add energy and bandwidth after the learning tapers.

This makes me think the most interesting uses of AI are likely going to be at relatively small scales. 2-3 Claude Max accounts/human perhaps. You’re pushing the AI harder than someone orchestrating 24. Chances are you’re doing relatively novel things instead of trying to one-shot a GTA6 clone (that would take scale but not much orchestration).

Another way to get at this conclusion is Brook’s Law. Adding humans to a late project delays it, due to the cost of bringing them into the context. Adding agents to a late project actually faces the same dilemma. A huge swarm has a corresponding swarm of local contexts. One way to make it easier us if the agents you’re adding are doing increasingly dumb things that are easy to integrate.
