Theory building, using computing as the test bed for two of the theory's
own open questions (reference/world-machines.md): the characteristic
pathologies of a Dusk phase, and whether World Machines have fractal
structure.

If the digital stack is Modernity's machinery, its pathologies should show
the Dusk signature — a machine increasingly unable to solve the problems it
used to handle, but still structurally present. Candidates to test against
the record: returns to scale that keep being promised as they shrink;
energy and water costs exported to places and people who don't share the
gains; standardisation that blocks the alternatives that would fix it. Do
these hold up, and are they distinctively Dusk, or what any mature system
looks like?

And fractal structure: does the stack itself show Dawn/Day/Dusk phases at a
smaller scale (mainframe, PC, cloud, AI), and does the timing line up with
the parent machine's? A comparison with Perez's techno-economic paradigms is
the obvious control: what does World Machines theory say here that Perez
doesn't?

Every finding is a claim, and a conjecture until something tests it.
Suggest tiny experiments where a step is checkable (e.g. energy per useful
operation over time, from public data).
