The PI's take argues that Liveness needs descent by experience: learning that
writes downward through the layers of a system, not only upward into its
context. Its contention is that a system can only get there if it descends as
fast as it ascends, and that every channel letting experience write downward is
also a channel for corruption. Biology's answer, on the take's reading, is not
to close the channels but to gate them (sleep, consolidation, the slow sorting
of what deserves to sink and what should evaporate). The take ends on the
question the lab should carry: can a system know what and why to forget?

Why it matters for the lab: it is Liveness material in the World Machines
vocabulary (reference/world-machines.md), and it is the other side of q-002's
"let bodies compute" reading. Two things to test, not assume:

- Does a gate actually stop a bad input from reaching the lower layers, and at
  what cost to what the system learns from good ones? A toy online learner with
  a two-timescale (fast context, slow weights) gate, against an ungated one, on a
  stream with rare poisoned inputs, would show this if pre-registered. Small
  enough for a runner.
- The take's own material (the sleep-framework write-up on continual learning,
  the tufazip and rlvrbook links) is where the "gating" is already being built in
  practice. Read it as material: what gate is it, and what does it forget?

Open questions for the next shift: is "descent at the rate of ascent" a
measurable thing (what is a layer, what is a rate)? And is the biology a
metaphor or a mechanism here (Wolff's law, autoimmunity, cancer as a local
override are analogies until something carries them over)? Say which, per claim.
