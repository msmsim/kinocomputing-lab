World Machines theory (https://worldmachines.org/theory) reads history through
the life cycles of world-scale machines: emergence, dominance, the costs they
impose, obsolescence. Treat the incumbent stack — von Neumann digital
computing, GPUs, scaling transformers — as one. What is its life-cycle stage,
on evidence (energy as the binding constraint, the end of Dennard scaling,
capital concentration)? What costs does it externalise? And do the two
clusters of alternatives in https://www.kinocomputing.com/alternative-paradigms
— "let physics compute" and "let bodies compute" — look like candidate
successor machines, or like components the incumbent will absorb?

Separate what the sources say from what the lab infers. Name what evidence
would change the conclusion. A deep run's work, not a light one's.
