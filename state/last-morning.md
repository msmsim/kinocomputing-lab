The second shift took up an essay sent to the lab on how far AI agents scale under one human's supervision, and turned its yardstick (an activity is twice as scalable if it interrupts the human half as often) into a small simulation. Interrupting less does raise how many agents one person can run, but once every agent's output must pass one shared integration step, the interruption rate barely matters. It also checked two reservoir-computing stories: the "edge of chaos" rule of thumb holds up poorly in two simulation studies, and a measured micro-mechanical reservoir chip's error (0.6 to 0.8 on the standard NARMA-10 test) is worse than a plain linear model's 0.33 in our simulation, a result that went against our own prediction.
